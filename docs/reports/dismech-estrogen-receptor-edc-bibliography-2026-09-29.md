# Verified literature on the estrogen receptor as an endocrine-disruptor screening target

**Date:** 2026-09-29
**For:** sessions working in this repository, and @gingin77
**Companion to:** [the AOP/ToxCast bridge note](dismech-estrogen-receptor-aop-toxcast-bridge-2026-09-26.md)

Forty-nine PubMed records, every identifier and title confirmed against the
PubMed summary API on 2026-09-29. Sixteen of them are cited in dismech issue
#12925; the rest were verified during the same research pass and used nowhere, so
this file is the only place they survive.

The point of writing them down is that verification is the expensive part. A PMID
recalled rather than looked up is the failure mode dismech's own curation rules
are built around, and re-running the searches costs a full research pass. Titles
below are as PubMed returned them, so they can be copied into a citation without
a second lookup. **Nothing here has been read in full.** A verified identifier
says the paper exists and is named this; it does not say the paper supports the
claim you want it for, and it is not a substitute for reading it before quoting it.

## The regulatory mandate

The statutory case that estrogen is the axis endocrine screening was built
around, rather than one axis among three.

statement of *why* this receptor rather than another: its ligand tolerance is
broad enough that structurally unrelated industrial chemicals bind it. The 1971
case series is indexed twice, as the original and as a 1999 reprint.

## What was searched for and not found

The most perishable part of a research pass, and the part nothing else records.
Each line below is a search that was run and came back empty. Re-running one is
cheap; discovering it needs running is not.

- **No publication calls this receptor the "archetypal" or "prototypical"
  endocrine-disruptor target.** Searched explicitly across titles and abstracts
  for that framing alongside "endocrine disrupt*". The claim has to be assembled
  from the components above, which is why dismech#12925 states it in the weaker
  form that the components actually carry.
- **No published source ranks ToxCast targets by endpoint count.** The figure of
  40 endpoints for this receptor traces to dismech issue #12858 itself, not to
  literature. Present it as computed from the assay annotation table, with the
  invitrodb version stated. What *is* citable is the within-programme comparison:
  18 assays in the estrogen model against 11 in the androgen model.
- **No PubMed-indexed article describes the OECD Conceptual Framework.** Six
  differently-phrased searches returned only papers that use it. Cite Guidance
  Document 150, or section 3 of Test Guideline 455.
- **Neither WHO report has a PMID or DOI.** Use the document identifiers above,
  or the 2013 consensus statement as a citable proxy.
- **The drinking-water half of the screening mandate was never confirmed from a
  primary document.** The 2015 Federal Register notice cites only the food-quality
  statute. If the claim is needed, source it separately.
- **The current status of Test Guideline 457 is unresolved.** Its method appears
  as an annex of Test Guideline 455, which suggests absorption, but no statement
  to that effect was found. Do not assert it was retired.
- **Earlier edition years of Test Guidelines 455 and 493 are unverified.** Only
  the current editions were read.
- **No separate final Federal Register notice of acceptance was found.** The
  operative statement that the agency accepts the model as an alternative to
  three Tier 1 assays is in the 2015 screening paper's own abstract.

## Method

Identifiers were collected during a research pass on 2026-09-26 and every one was
re-confirmed on 2026-09-29 against `esummary.fcgi` in two batches, taking the
title, year and journal from the response rather than from the collecting pass.
Titles appear as returned, with a trailing period stripped. Four identifiers that
the original pass could not resolve are deliberately absent rather than guessed:
a 1993 rainbow trout hepatocyte assay, a yeast two-hybrid screen of 517
chemicals, a 2001 structure-activity paper, and a 2007 bisphenol A consensus
statement. They may well be indexed; the queries used did not find them.
